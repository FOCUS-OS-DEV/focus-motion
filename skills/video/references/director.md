# The director's plan

Read this before planning any full video (the `idea`, `voice` and `footage` tracks). Single jobs such as a
transcript, captions only, a voice clean-up or organising media need no plan.

The plan decides what the viewer feels, second by second, before anything is built. A video assembled from a
checklist of effects feels mechanical. A directed video has a few moments people remember. The work here is to
choose those moments, then take each one as far as it can go.

## 1. One sentence first

Write the one thing the viewer should think or do when the video ends. Every later choice serves it. If the request
does not make it clear, it is one of the brief questions.

## 2. The viewer's journey

Lay the video out as three to six beats. For each beat, write what the viewer should feel. What is on screen comes
later.

| Beat | The viewer |
|---|---|
| Hook, the first two or three seconds | stops scrolling and wants to know what this is |
| Set-up | understands the subject and that it concerns them |
| Build | sees that it is real: proof, how it works |
| Peak | is surprised or delighted, the moment they would show a friend |
| Payoff | gets the point in one line |
| Ask | knows exactly what to do next |

A short video needs fewer. Ten seconds can be hook, peak, ask.

## 3. Choose the peaks

Pick the few moments that carry the video: about one for every ten to fifteen seconds, and the hook is always one of
them. The others are usually the main promise, the proof and the ask.

Peaks get a real idea and most of the work. Everything between them supports: clear words, calm motion, room to
breathe. Do not give every sentence its own big idea. When every line performs, nothing stands out and the video
loses its soul.

## 4. Find the idea for each peak

The idea comes from what this video says. Ask: if this line were a moving picture, what would it be?

Ways in, as inspiration and never as a menu:
- Show the real thing: the user's product, screen, people or footage.
- Let a comparison play out: for "prices keep climbing", a number that climbs until it breaks its frame.
- Make the words act: a word about speed arrives fast, and a "no" pushes the line off the screen.
- A change the viewer watches happen: a mess becoming order, before becoming after.
- A reveal: something turns, opens or lands.
- A count that lands on its number.
- A small character or object that reacts.
- A callback: something from the hook returns at the end.

Three tests for an idea:
1. Could it sit in any other video unchanged? Then it is generic. Go back to the line.
2. Did this video already use the same device? Use a different one.
3. Does it need explaining? Then it is too clever. Simplify.

## 5. The look

Each video gets its own look. Carry a look over from an earlier project only when the brand kit asks for it.

- **Colours.** Use the brand kit when there is one. Otherwise propose three to five colours that fit the subject,
  one of them an accent.
- **Type.** One family from `assets/fonts/fonts.json` carries most of the text. Add a second only for contrast.
- **World.** Choose one and keep it through the video: flat and bold, paper and ink, soft 3D, glass and light, pixel
  art, editorial.
- **Motion character.** Snappy, calm, playful or heavy. It sets the eases and the timing.

When the choice is a matter of taste, offer the user two directions, one line each, and recommend one.

## 6. The pace map

- **The hook is the fastest part.** Something new arrives about every half second.
- **The body.** Something new arrives roughly every second. "New" means a change the viewer notices: an element
  enters, the shot cuts, a word lands with a hit, a screen changes. A slow drift does not count.
- **Breathing stretches.** They may run up to two seconds with gentle motion. They are never frozen.
- **The last message.** It stays readable for at least a second and a half.
- **With a voice.** On the `voice` and `footage` tracks, each event starts on a spoken word, at the moment the word
  starts.

## 7. Sound

Mark where the two or three biggest hits land and where silence helps. Music is used only when the user brings a
file they are allowed to use.

## 8. Truth

- Numbers, prices, names and claims come from the user. Never invent one. Ask, or leave it out.
- Every word that will appear on screen is listed in the plan with its exact spelling.
- If the video promises something, such as "comment and get the guide", ask whether it exists yet.

## 9. What the user sees

Show the plan in Hebrew, short enough to read in a minute. The words in angle brackets are filled in:

```
**התוכנית לסרטון** (<אורך>, <פורמט>)

**המסר:** <משפט אחד>

| זמן | מה נאמר או כתוב | מה רואים | מה הצופה מרגיש |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | <...> | <...> | <...> |

**הרגעים הגדולים:** <שניים עד ארבעה, שורה לכל אחד>
**המראה:** <צבעים, פונט, עולם ואופי התנועה, בשתי שורות>
**סאונד:** <אפקטים, מוזיקה>
**כל המילים שיופיעו על המסך:** <רשימה>
**מה אני צריך מכם:** <קבצים, מספרים, החלטות>

מאשרים, או שיש מה לשנות?
```

- **Describe what is seen.** Use plain words. Internal terms such as easing, timeline, composition or keyframe stay
  out of the plan.
- **Two looks.** When there are two, add a line for each above the question, marked א and ב, and say which one you
  recommend.
- **Wait for a clear yes.** Nothing is built before it. When the user asks for changes, fix the plan and show it again.
- **Save it.** Write the approved plan to `plan.md` in the project folder.

## 10. The footage track

The person on camera is the source. Their face, lips and voice are never regenerated or altered. The plan is an edit
plan:

1. **Open on the strongest sentence.** Find it in the transcript and put it in the first two seconds.
2. **The cut list.** State which sentences stay and in what order, what is removed (false starts, repeats, dead air)
   and how many seconds that saves.
3. **Where graphics appear.** Give them to the peaks only. Between the peaks the person simply talks.
4. **Captions.** State whether they are on, and where they sit.
5. **Covering cuts.** Use the user's photos and extra clips, or a full-screen graphic moment.

The user approves the transcript first, then this plan.

## 11. After approval

The plan is the contract.
- **A small change** found while building: make the smallest fix that works, and tell the user in one line when you
  show the result. An example is a closing line that comes out too small.
- **A larger change**, such as a different scene or different words, goes back for approval.
